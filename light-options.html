1 · Flat white document

Everything white, cards separated by a hairline. No tinted layers at all.

Reads like a printed financial document. Calmest and most institutional — but the cards can feel undifferentiated on a big screen.

2 · Pale blue surface your suggestion

White page, a soft blue app surface, white cards floating on it.

My recommendation. The tint gives cards something to sit on so they read as objects, while staying light enough for long sessions. Closest to how the dark theme actually feels.

3 · Deep blue band high contrast

White page, a saturated blue app panel, white cards lifted off it with shadow.

Cards pop hardest and it is unmistakably branded — but section headings must go white, so it is really a third theme to maintain, not light mode.

4 · Grey paper + cyan rail accented

Soft grey page, white cards, a cyan rail down the leading edge of each card.

The rail is where brand cyan earns its place — as a fill it needs no contrast. Good, though a rail on every card gets noisy; better reserved for the active section.

5 · Blue-bordered cards your suggestion

All white, with a full blue border drawn around every card.

Defines cards without any tint — but with a dozen cards per screen the borders start to read as a cage. Works far better if only the active card gets the blue.

6 · Near-white paper subtle

White page, a barely-there paper surface with a hairline edge, white cards on top.

The safest middle ground — separation without any colour commitment. Least likely to age badly, least distinctive.
My recommendation: 2 (pale blue surface), with the rail from 4 used only on the active section. It gives cards a ground to sit on so they read as objects rather than floating text, keeps the whole screen comfortable for long sessions, and reserves brand cyan for fills where its poor contrast does not matter.

What I would avoid: option 3 as your default — a saturated blue band forces headings to white, so you end up maintaining a third theme rather than a light one. And option 5 everywhere: a blue border on every card reads as a cage once there are ten of them on screen.

Note the header stays dark in every option. That is deliberate and already how the app behaves — it anchors the page and keeps the logo working. Say if you would rather it went light too, since that changes the whole feel.
