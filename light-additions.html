Fundable · light mode — step 2c

Clean additions on top of 2 & 6.

Both your picks are the same family — a subtle tinted ground with white cards. So the differentiation has to come from what sits on that ground. Six additions, each shown applied to the base so you can judge it rather than imagine it.

Everything here is additive: pick any combination. Base shown is option 2 (pale blue surface); all work equally on 6.

A1 · Cyan top-rule on cards

A 2px cyan rule fading out across the top edge of each card, instead of a left rail or full border.

More architectural than a rail and it never becomes a cage — the fade stops it repeating heavily down the page. This is where cyan works as a fill.

A2 · Step spine

A hairline runs down the left of the app surface, with a cyan node at each numbered step.

The most distinctive of the six and it encodes something true — your app is a numbered sequence. It also makes the 01 → 02 → 03 flow visible while scrolling.

B1 · Funded vs your-cash tinting

Figures the government or levy covers sit on a cool tint; figures that cost you real cash sit on a warm one.

My strongest recommendation. It is the only addition that does real work — a CFO sees "what I pay" versus "what is covered" without reading a single label. Colour reinforces the text, never replaces it.

C2 · Elevation on the active card

Cards sit flat at rest; the section you are working in lifts slightly.

Adds hierarchy without any colour, which matters in light mode where you have far less contrast budget than in dark. Subtle enough to never be noticed consciously.
My recommended stack
  1. Base 2 — pale blue surface, white cards (or base 6 if you want it quieter)
  2. B1 funded vs your-cash tinting — the only addition that carries meaning; do this one even if you do nothing else
  3. A2 step spine — the distinctive one, and honest to the app's numbered structure
  4. B2 cyan as state — pairs with the spine; keeps cyan meaningful rather than decorative
  5. C1 zebra rows — free legibility once tables get long
What I would leave out: A1 (top-rule) if you take A2 — spine and rule both mark structure, and running both is one signal too many. C2 is the most optional: real, but you would struggle to notice it in a screenshot comparison.

One caution on B1. Tinting by meaning is powerful, so it has to be correct — a figure tinted "covered" that the employer actually pays would be worse than no tint at all. It should be driven by the same engine that produces the numbers, not applied by hand per card.
